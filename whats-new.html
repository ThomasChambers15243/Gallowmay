0.8.0

Features

  • Reference Manager is now called Gallowmay. The same app under a new name, with a new website: https://thomaschambers15243.github.io/Gallowmay/. Updating from Reference Manager keeps your library, papers and settings where they are. On Windows Gallowmay takes Reference Manager's place: its shortcuts, and a pin on the taskbar, open Gallowmay, and Reference Manager is uninstalled. On a Mac the app takes the name Gallowmay in Applications when it next quits after the update. Backups, works packages and git repositories made by Reference Manager open as before.
  • Go to anything: Ctrl+P (⌘P on a Mac). One box for going anywhere without the mouse. With nothing typed it lists the sidebar's pages (with the extensions turned on) and the pinned projects, then the commands that work on any page, each with its key. Type to narrow them, and to find up to five each of your references (the library's search, filters and all), papers (one found by the words inside it shows the passage and opens at its page), notes in every project and the library, people, venues and projects. Enter opens what is chosen, and Ctrl+Enter (⌘Return on a Mac), Ctrl+click or the middle button opens it in a new tab behind; a reference opens in the form, and a command runs as its key would, including those that have no key, such as Close the other tabs and New reference. Escape, or Ctrl+P again, closes it, and Settings → Keyboard shortcuts gives it another key.
  • Cite from anywhere. Give Cite from anywhere a key in Settings → Keyboard shortcuts (under Anywhere on the computer) and pressing it in any program, while Gallowmay is open, brings up a small window over it. Search the library with its filters, or a project's references with its keys; move with the arrows, tick with Enter or a click, and press Ctrl+Enter (⌘Return on a Mac) to finish. It writes the project's cite command (\citep{a,b}), Pandoc ([@a; @b]), Typst (@a @b), the citation in the text in a style ("(Shannon, 1948; Gallager, 1962)"), or the formatted references, and remembers your choice, style and project. Finishing copies it, says Copied: paste it and closes the window; on a Mac Gallowmay then steps aside, so the keyboard goes back to the program you were in. Escape closes it without copying. The key needs Ctrl, Alt or ⌘ (or is a function key), and one the system, another program or Gallowmay itself already uses is refused.
  • Citations in the text, formatted in any style. Where the app writes citations in the text (the cite picker, a note exported as a document, and programs that use Connections from other programs), they are formatted together with the reference list, so two works by one author in one year are 2020a and 2020b in both, a numbered style numbers by first citation, and a note style cites a work in full once and shortly after. Pages and other places ("p. 4", "pp. 3–5", "chap. 2", "§ 3") are printed as the style names them, with the words written before and after them.
  • Notes into documents. Export as a document… in a note's menu (or Export… above the note) writes the note out with its citations formatted in the citation style you choose and the reference list at the end. A web page needs nothing else: one file with the pictures of marked areas inside it, easy to read on screen and to print. With Pandoc 2.11 or later installed (pandoc.org) it can be a Word or LibreOffice document or a PDF too, and the dialog says which Pandoc it found and what it makes PDFs with. Citations are read as Pandoc reads them: [see @a, p. 4; @b], @a as part of a sentence ("Shannon (1948)"), @a [p. 4], [-@a] without the author's name, and the keys under nocite: at the top. A style that cites in notes puts them in footnotes. A project's notes cite by the project's keys; a citation with a key no reference has is printed as typed, and the keys missing are named.
  • Check document reads Markdown and Typst documents. A project's document can now be the main file of a Markdown document (Pandoc's .md, Quarto's .qmd, R Markdown's .Rmd) or a Typst one (.typ), as well as a .tex or .aux file. Citations are read as Pandoc reads them ([@key], @key, @{key}, and the keys under nocite: at the top, where @* uses every reference) or as Typst does (@key, #cite(<key>)), and Quarto's {{< include >}} and Typst's #include are followed. Code, comments and cross-references (@fig-plot, @sec:intro, or in Typst a label the document gives) are not taken for citations. Keys are renamed in these files as in .tex files, after a preview and with a .bak copy of each; in Markdown a key that cannot follow an @ as it is goes in braces.
  • Type its reference… fills in what the paper's first page says. The form beside an unlinked paper opens with its printed title, a DOI printed on the first page (one written after "doi" or "doi.org" first), and an arXiv paper's number, read from the stamp arXiv prints in the margin (a preprint only cited there is not taken for the paper). A paper with an arXiv stamp and no DOI opens as the preprint, a miscellaneous reference with arXiv as its eprint archive and the year it was sent in. A note above the form says what was filled in, to check against the paper; nothing is looked up online, and nothing is saved until you save it. Arriving from the Papers page, the form waits a moment for the paper's text to be read.
  • Library health. Library health, a button on the library page with how many things it found, opens a page of what would make a .bib file written from your library wrong, each check a section with its count and each finding with what fixes it. References that may be the same (alike in every field, with the same title, or with titles at least 90% alike, as the reference form warns) open side by side with Open both, field by field with the differences marked, to edit one or delete the other. People who may be one ("J. Smith" and "John Smith", "J. R. Tolkien" and "John Ronald Tolkien") and venues that may be one ("IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory" typed as a venue of its own) have Merge…. A venue that a template in use would abbreviate but that has no abbreviation with the tag it asks for has Add an abbreviation…. References without a field their type needs, titles whose capitals a BibTeX template that does not protect titles leaves the style to lower ("DNA", "Bayesian", with the words named), arXiv preprints that name no journal and have no other version, and PDFs of references that are not on this computer each have Open. Not a problem hides a finding, and the page can show those again. Go to library health can be given a key in Settings. Nothing is looked up online.
  • Bringing a library over from Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley and others. Import a Zotero library… (on the Works packages page, and on the empty library) reads Zotero's data folder from a copy of its database, so Zotero can stay open. Its items become references, with Zotero's fields given BibLaTeX's names as Better BibTeX gives them, and the citation keys Zotero or Better BibTeX gave them where there are any. Its collections become the project's groups and categories, its tags tags, its notes Markdown notes, the PDFs it stores the references' PDFs, and the highlights, underlines and notes in them the PDFs' own, in their colours. What is in Zotero's trash, linked files, other files and other kinds of marks are left out, and the preview counts them. RIS and CSL-JSON files import as a .bib file does, their keywords becoming tags; an RIS file's notes become notes of the project, and the PDFs it links to come too.
  • Check later. Importing a package, a file or a Zotero library can put all its references into your library at once, marked not checked yet, instead of keeping them in its project until each is checked: tick Bring them all into the library, not checked yet, which asks first. They are used as any other reference, with a badge in the library table and the form, is:unchecked and is:checked in the search, and a % line above each in the .bib files written. Check them now on the library page opens each in the form, with Skip this one and Stop. Saving one to the library, there or anywhere else, checks it. One with the title of a reference already in your library stays in the project, to be told apart.
  • Highlights and notes made in another reader come in with the PDF. A PDF highlighted in Acrobat, Preview or another reference manager says so over the page: "This PDF has 12 highlights and 3 notes made in another reader", with Bring them in and Not now, which is remembered for the paper (its Details still offers them). Highlights, underlines and strike-throughs keep their colours, comments and the dates they were made, and each takes the words under it from the page. Sticky notes become notes where they were, and a box of text becomes a note at its corner saying what it said. Replies join the comment of what they answer. They are not on the timeline, as you did not make them now. Once brought in they are the paper's own notes, and the PDF's copies are no longer drawn under them. Bringing them in again adds nothing; one you delete is not brought back unless you choose Bring them in again in Details. The message after bringing them in has an Undo that offers them again. The app's own highlights, saved into a copy of a PDF, are never brought in. The PDF file itself is never changed.
  • Scanned papers can be read. Turn on Settings → Scanned papers → Read the text of scanned papers, and the computer's own text recognition (Apple's Vision on a Mac, Windows' own on Windows) reads the pictures of a scan's pages in the background, offline. The scan is then found by the words inside it, called by its printed title, and its reference list read for "cited by", the citation graph and the viewer's References panel. Finding words in the viewer (Ctrl+F) and selecting its text do not reach it yet. Each computer reads its own copies; the paper's Details says when its text was read this way.
  • Quicker with a large library. Opening, saving and checking a reference for duplicates read only the references they need rather than the whole library (one reference loads about 15 times quicker in a library of 3,000), a search inside papers fetches all its passages at once, and the library waits less for the disk as it saves.
  • Papers read in the background no longer refresh every window one by one. A run of papers read, or added from a watched folder, is one refresh every few seconds, and each paper's text, title and reference list are kept in one step.
  • Typing notes on a paper no longer refreshes the app at every pause. They are saved as they are typed, and the rest of the app catches up once you leave them.
  • The window opens without waiting for the copy of the library kept at each start, and copies of the library (backups, Copy the database only…, the Git extension's commits) no longer hold up the app while they are made.
  • A project's page asks only for its own references' PDFs, not every paper in the library, and the Papers page asks for its list once rather than twice.
  • Deleting several papers, and adding every reference a document cites, each happen in one step: all of them, or none.
  • Highlights rolled into one note. Two or more highlights that read as one passage, on one page or across several, become one note: choose Roll in a highlight's note and click another highlight (or select text to highlight it and roll it in), use Roll with the note above or below in a note's menu in the Notes panel, or drag a note there by its handle onto another. A curved line in the page's margin joins the highlights, going from one colour to the next when they differ, and the note shows their text as one passage, with one comment. A note of several colours has a little tag of each along its top, and a border of them all. Each highlight keeps its own colour and kind, changed from its note, and its ends can still be dragged. The scissors on the line separate the note there, and Take out and Separate all in the note take one highlight or all of them apart; Split, in Adjust, cuts a highlight in two where you click, between two words. A highlight made across pages is now one note, each page's part with its own text (it was a highlight on the first page and "…" on the others). Deleting a rolled note deletes all of it, and Undo brings it back rolled. Notes saved or copied as Markdown, To a note…, From a paper…, works packages and the Git extension's copy each take a rolled note as one; a copy of the PDF saved with its highlights has each of them, with the comment on the first.
  • An outline of a note's headings. The Outline button above a note lists its headings beside it, indented by level: a click goes there in whichever view shows (the caret to the heading in the writing and live views, the reading side scrolled to it), and the heading you are under is marked as you write or scroll. Headings are lines starting with #, or underlined with === or ---; a # in a block of code is not one. The panel is remembered, can be widened, and Outline can be given a key in Settings.
  • Pinned tabs and tab groups. Right-click a tab and choose Pin to keep it at the start of the strip as its icon alone. A pinned tab stays open: Ctrl+W, the middle button and Close the other tabs leave it, and only Close in its menu closes it. Add to a new group puts tabs together under a coloured name. Click the name to fold its tabs away and again to show them, double-click to rename it, and right-click it to change its colour, ungroup it or close the group. A tab dropped on a tab of a group, or on the group's name (folded away or not), goes into the group; dropped on a tab in no group, or past the last tab, it leaves its group. The tab being dragged shows the colour of the group it would go into. A page opened from a tab in a group opens in the group. The window keeps its pinned tabs and groups the next time it opens. Pin or unpin the tab can be given a key in Settings.
  • Anything that opens a page opens in a new tab with the middle button, and with Ctrl+click (⌘ on a Mac), behind the tab you are in: the sidebar's pages and projects, project and sketch cards, people and venues and the links on their pages, the notes a search finds, the PDFs, notes, projects and papers named in the reference form, a paper's events on the timeline, the papers that cite one in a paper's details, and the PDF buttons of the citation graph, the citation card and the sketch board. A right-click on them offers Open and Open in a new tab.
  • Undo in the viewer. Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) undoes the last change to a paper's notes: a highlight, note or marked area made, a change of colour, kind or of what is marked, a note deleted, highlights rolled together, taken apart or split. Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y does it again, and so do the two buttons beside Back. A highlight made across pages is one step, its parts and their roll together. What is typed in a comment is undone in its box, as before. The Undo a deletion offers brings the note back as it was, and the keys can be changed in Settings.
  • Programs on this computer can reach your library. A new extension, Connections from other programs, off until turned on, lets the browser connector, or another program on this computer, search the library, list a project's keys, write citations (as the project's cite command, for Pandoc, for Typst, or in the text in any citation style, a whole document's at once so that they agree) and formatted references, open the cite picker and be handed what you choose there, add a PDF to Papers, and send BibTeX that opens in the review form for you to check. It listens on this computer only (127.0.0.1, port 23125 unless you choose another), and each program needs a token made on the extension's page, shown once and kept only as a fingerprint. Web pages cannot reach it: in a browser, only browser extensions may call it, each with its token. The page shows whether it is listening, the programs given a token (with when each was last used, and Forget…) and the last 20 requests.
  • A browser connector. In Chrome, Edge or Firefox, its button reads the paper you are reading (the tags publishers write, schema.org, Dublin Core, COinS, or a DOI or arXiv number in the address) and says what it found. Send to Gallowmay fetches the PDF the page names with your own login, adds it to Papers, and opens the reference in the review form, to be linked to that PDF when you save it; the first time a PDF comes from a site, the browser asks to let it reach that site (refused, the reference goes without its PDF). It pairs with the app by the port and a token from the Connections page, and sends nothing anywhere else. It is built and loaded on its own, from connector/ (not in the stores yet).
  • Look a reference up by its identifier, an extension. Turned on in Settings → Extensions (it goes online, and says where), Add by identifier… in the library, on its page and in Go to anything takes a DOI, an arXiv number, an ISBN or a PubMed id, typed or pasted, or a link that holds one (doi.org, arXiv, PubMed, Open Library, or a publisher's address with a DOI in it). It asks doi.org (Crossref's DOIs and DataCite's alike), arXiv, Open Library or PubMed, and opens what it finds in the review form, "Looked up at Crossref", to check against the paper; nothing is saved by itself. In the reference form, Look it up beside a DOI, or an eprint that is an arXiv number or a PubMed id, offers what the record has for each field the form leaves empty or holds otherwise, to take one by one or all at once; Type its reference… fills the DOI and the arXiv number from a paper's first page, ready for it.
  • Feeds, an extension and a prototype: an inbox of new papers. Follow arXiv categories on its page, a person with Follow on OpenAlex… on their page (you choose which of OpenAlex's authors they are, matched by the references of theirs in your library or by name), and a reference with Watch for papers citing it in its right-click menu in the library. Once a day, and with Look now, it brings the newest papers in each category, new work by the people you follow and new papers citing the references you watch, leaving out what your library already has. Each paper shows by feed with its authors, date and why it is there, and offers Review… (it opens in the review form for you to check), Open and Not for me. What you decided goes with the library, the papers themselves stay on this computer, and nothing is added by itself. It is a prototype, and says so beside its switch and on its page: the idea is good, but what it brings is not yet fine-grained enough, since an arXiv category alone brings far more new papers a day than anyone can read.
  • Works packages can be followed at their web address, and updated when a new version comes out. A new extension, Works packages from the web, off until you turn it on in Settings. Subscribe to a package at any https address of its .json or .zip, or pick one from the list on Gallowmay's website with Find packages online…. It imports as any package does. The app looks for a new version once a week (or when you click Look now), and the package's card shows Update…. Before anything changes, the update shows what it would change: references added, changed field by field, and taken out; groups, categories, tags, notes and PDFs. What you made yours stays. References you brought into your library are not changed; the package's changes to them go into a note to compare. Notes you edited stay, with the new text in a note beside them. References you moved stay in their group, and PDFs you highlighted stay.
  • A person's notes are a profile, written in Markdown. On a person's page, the section under their references over the years shows their references or your notes about them, chosen with References and Notes at its top. The notes are written as a note's live view writes Markdown, reading as they are typed: headings, lists, links, and @key citing a reference in your library. They are saved a moment after you stop typing.
  • Export a reference from its menu. Right-click a reference in the library, a project, Papers, or on a person's or a venue's page (or open the viewer's menu) for Export: Copy BibTeX, Copy as the citation style you chose last (APA to begin with, with its italics, for a word processor), BibTeX, RIS, CSL-JSON… to copy or save in any format and, for a .bib, with any template, and In another citation style…. From a project it is written as the project writes it, with the project's key.
  • A click on a paper shows its reference; a double click opens the paper. In Papers and a project's papers, a click shows the paper's reference beside the list, so clicking down the list shows each one's; a paper not linked yet asks which reference it is. A double click opens the paper, and the middle button opens it in a new tab.
  • Paste a paper's BibTeX into its reference. Paste its BibTeX…, beside Type its reference…, opens the paper with the reference form beside it and a box for the BibTeX: it fills the form in, as Paste BibTeX in the library does, for you to check against the paper and finish, and saving links the paper. Every new reference's form has Paste BibTeX… at its top.
  • Search related, an extension. Works like a paper, by its authors, on its subject or in its venue, or any mix, at several places at once: OpenAlex and arXiv in the app, each work to add as a reference (through the review form), as a PDF, or both, the PDF linked once the reference is saved; Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, PubMed, dblp, Crossref and places you add yourself in browser tabs. Search related… in a reference's or a paper's menu, and on a person's or a venue's page, starts from it. It goes online only when you search.
  • Several tabs at once. Ctrl+click (⌘+click on a Mac) chooses tabs one by one, and Shift+click every tab between. Right-click one of those chosen to pin or unpin them, put them in a new group or one there is, take them out of their groups, move them to a new window, duplicate them or close them; drag one of them and the others come along, into a group, to another place in the strip, or out to another window.
  • A venue's notes are written in Markdown too, under its references over the years, as a person's are.
  • Export in a reference's form. Wherever a reference opens (a click on it, or on a paper, in a list), Export at the top of its form copies or saves it, as its right-click menu does.
  • A Linux build, not tried yet. GitHub now builds a .deb and an AppImage beside the Windows and Mac versions; they are kept with each build for 30 days rather than published, and nothing about them can hold up a release. On Linux, links open in the browsers installed there (or as xdg-open chooses), the Git extension keeps its token or passphrase in the desktop's keyring (GNOME Keyring or KWallet), and the keys Linux desktops keep for themselves cannot be given to commands. The app says when a new version is out, but does not install it itself there.
  • Typing practice. An extension: a typing trainer that drills you on prose from your own papers and notes, measures your speed and accuracy, and shows which keys slow you down. Add from the library… on its page, or Practise typing on this in a paper's or a note's menu, gives it a copy, which its page shows at once; files added in its own Library stay there. What it keeps stays apart from the library, and the Git extension keeps it with your library's history.
  • What's new, once. After an update, a short list of what the version brought, each with a way to try it, and its new extensions to turn on there and then.
  • The library format changes (to 19). Updating from an earlier version saves a copy of your library in backups\ first.

Bug fixes

  • **category: in the library's search finds what is in the categories below one,** as clicking the category does and as the papers' search already did: category:thesis finds a reference filed in Thesis → Chapter 2. It found only references filed in Thesis itself. The library's list of filters also says that tag: finds a reference by its own tags, not only its PDF's.
  • The citation graph is drawn. Its picture could be left with no size, showing nothing: the area it is drawn in was measured only once, and when the graph was still being read it was not there to measure. It is measured from when it appears.
  • A note given a colour that is not shown stays in view. With only some colours shown (the colours at the top of the Notes panel), a highlight or marked area given another colour vanished from the page and the panel as if deleted; its colour is now shown too, as a new note's already was.

0.7.17

Features

  • Git, an extension. Your library in a git repository of your own, everything but the PDFs: a history to go back through, and the same library on every computer. Turned on in Settings → Extensions, it sits just above Settings in the sidebar. Give it an empty repository (GitHub, anywhere git is served, or an empty folder on a shared drive) and sign in with a username and token, an SSH key (one you have, or one made for the computer) or nothing; the secret goes into the computer's keychain. Left to itself it pulls when the app starts, commits and pushes a little after changes settle and when the app closes; the page also commits, pushes, pulls, makes and switches branches, merges, goes back to any earlier version, and takes typed git commands (with the git program on the computer, when there is one). When both this computer and the repository changed the library, you choose which to keep; the other waits on a branch. A commit holds the database less what belongs to one computer (linked files, watched folders) and less the text read out of PDFs, the pictures of marked areas, and a readable copy: .bib files, notes as Markdown, highlights as JSON. The git library is built into the app; nothing needs installing.
  • The PDFs can be kept in another folder. Settings → Your data → Keep the PDFs in another folder… moves them there and keeps them there from then on: a folder that Google Drive, Dropbox or iCloud syncs holds the same PDFs on every computer, and the Git extension carries everything else. A paper whose PDF is not on this computer says so; adding the same file again links it back to its highlights.
  • Projects are in the sidebar only while pinned. The Pin switch on a project's card on the Projects page, or Pin to the sidebar in its menu, puts it in the sidebar, where it opens like a folder as before; off, it leaves. The others are reached from the Projects page, and a project's menu now opens its notes as well as its timeline. No project is pinned to begin with, those from earlier versions included.
  • The library format changes (to 12). Updating from an earlier version saves a copy of your library in backups\ first.

0.7.16

Features

  • Extensions. Features not everyone needs are built in but stay out of the way until turned on in Settings → Extensions, one switch each. One turned on joins the sidebar like any page, just above Projects, with its key for moving around and its page in each project's folder. Turned off, it leaves the sidebar and its tabs close (one in another window says it is off, and shows it again once it is back on), but it keeps its settings and its place for when it is turned on again. An extension that would let references into the library some other way than typing and checking them says so beside its switch, and asks before it is turned on.
  • The citation graph is the first extension. It is off until turned on, and has a new icon. A project's citation graph follows it, in the project's folder in the sidebar and its menu, and the graph remembers its two switches. A tab left at the graph while it is off says so, and leads to the switch.
  • The sidebar in any order. Settings → Sidebar lists its pages: drag one to move it, across the project folders to put it in the other section, and Put the sidebar back in its usual order undoes it all. Each section shows its pages and up to two extensions; with more, it scrolls.
  • How far the reference lists have been read, with Read them all again, is in Settings → Citations between papers as well as on the graph.

Bug fixes

  • Settings opened at a section, as Change them in Settings in the list of keys opens it, stays at that section while the sections above it fill in; it could end up some way above it.
  • In a short window the sidebar's lower pages were cut off, Settings among them. Each part of the sidebar now scrolls, and the projects keep a little room.
  • The citation graph was laid out afresh each time the reading of reference lists moved on, losing its zoom and the dots moved by hand; now only a change in the papers or their citations does that.

0.7.15

Features

  • Windows can be made much narrower. The smallest a window can be goes from 980×600 to 440×320, so two fit side by side on a laptop: a paper on the right, the notes on the left. In a narrow window the sidebar shows icons only, and widening it lays it over the page until a page is chosen. In a tighter one a page's own sidebar (categories and tags, the list of notes, a project's groups) folds away behind a button and lays over the page when opened. The templates page puts its preview under the template when there is no room beside it. Tabs that do not fit scroll sideways, with the mouse wheel too, keeping the one shown in view.
  • The middle button opens things in a new tab. Project cards, a project's PDFs and sketches, a package's project, the projects and PDFs named in the reference form and the notes a search finds join the lists that already did (the sidebar, papers, notes, people, venues, sketches). A middle click on a link in a note does what a click does.
  • A live view for notes. Besides writing, reading and the two side by side, a note can be written as it reads: headings, lists, quotes, bold and italic text and code take their look as you type, the marks (#, **, -) showing only on the line you are on; citations show as the references they cite, a task's box is ticked in place and the pictures of marked areas show. Ctrl+click (⌘ on a Mac) opens a link. The same keys work there, @ offers references as before, and From a paper… and Cite… put their text at the caret. The four views are one note: switch between them as you like, and the view is remembered. Live view can be given a key in Settings.
  • Papers and references share categories and tags. One set of categories and tags organises the library and the papers, and a paper linked to a reference is wherever its reference is: filing either files both, and a paper not linked to a reference is filed on its own. Settings → Categories and tags separates them again, each side keeping a copy of what there is, and brings them together by name. Updating from an earlier version brings the references' categories and tags together with the papers': a linked paper's own go to its reference, so the reference's other PDFs have them too. The library's category: and tag: filters find a reference by its own categories and tags too.
  • Projects have papers, as the library does. Drag a paper from Papers onto a project in the sidebar, or use Add to project… on it or on the papers ticked, to put it in the project; it stays in Papers. A project's page shows its References or its Papers, the latter with the Papers page's sidebar, search and menus: its own papers and its references' PDFs, one put in by hand taken out with a right-click. Add PDFs… there, or PDFs dropped on the window while the project is open, go into the project too. References dragged from the library onto a project in the sidebar are added to it. Project cards count the papers, and project: in the papers' search finds them. A works package made from the project carries its references' PDFs and says how many papers put in by hand it leaves out.
  • A reference's PDFs are versions, in an order. A preprint, the published version and a supplement are dragged into the order wanted among the reference form's PDFs, numbered; the first is the one every PDF button opens. The viewer's Version menu goes to the others. Until an order is chosen, the first added comes first.
  • Delete from a right-click. A reference in the library or a project (Remove from the project there too), a paper, a category and a tag each have Delete in their right-click menu.
  • A deleted category leaves what was in it to its parent. Its papers, references, people or venues go to the category it was in, and so do its sub-categories. Deleting a top-level category with sub-categories asks whether they go too (the choice offered first) or stay as top-level categories, with Don't ask again; Settings → Categories and tags keeps the choice and changes it. 0.7.14

Features

  • Import from other reference managers. Export your references from Zotero, Mendeley, JabRef, Paperpile or Overleaf as a .bib file and import it on the Works packages page (or drop it on the window). It becomes a project, as a works package does: the references keep their keys, the PDFs their file fields name come with them, keywords become tags, and JabRef's and Mendeley's groups become the project's groups and categories. Nothing goes into your library until you have checked it. An empty library offers it too.
  • Checking a package's references is easier. A name or a venue that came with the package and is not in your library yet is marked new, with the people or venues of yours it may be ("Smith, J." may be your "Smith, John", "IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory" your "IEEE Transactions on Information Theory"), one click from using yours. A PDF whose printed title differs from the reference's is pointed out.
  • Tips while checking. When you start checking a package's references, a note in the corner says what to look out for. Don't show this again turns it off, and Settings → Works packages turns it back on.
  • Unchecked references are marked. A reference that is not checked yet is marked so in the .bib files written from its project, with a % line above it, and the export says how many there are. In the project it is marked not checked yet.
  • From a paper… has a key. In a note, Ctrl+Shift+P (⌘⇧P on a Mac) opens it.
  • Arrow keys in search lists. The arrow keys and Enter choose from the lists in From a paper…, Add from library, choosing a paper's reference, To a note… and the notes search. In From a paper…, Ctrl+Enter adds what is ticked.
  • Markdown notes take a code editor's keys. Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+Shift+X and Ctrl+E make the selection, or the word at the caret, bold, italic, struck through or code, and take it off again. Ctrl+K makes it a link, and so does a web address pasted over it. Ctrl+Shift+8, Ctrl+Shift+7 and Ctrl+Shift+9 make the lines a list, a numbered list or a task list, and Ctrl+' a quote. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the lines, with Shift they copy them, and Ctrl+Shift+K deletes them, a numbered list staying numbered in order. A bracket, a quote mark or a mark such as *, _ or ~ typed over a selection goes round it instead of replacing it. Enter on a list item starts the next one, numbered on (an empty one ends the list), and Tab and Shift+Tab move it in and out. A paper's notes written in Markdown take the same keys. Ctrl+Z takes back each change, and a citation put in with @ or Cite…, or what From a paper… brings in, too. The write, read and side-by-side views can be given keys. On a Mac, ⌘ does what Ctrl does.
  • Keys for reading a paper. V, H, U, N, B, E and F choose a tool (select, highlight, underline, note, box, ellipse, freehand), and 1 to 8 a colour of the paper's colour group, as its colour button shows. Alt+Down and Alt+Up go to the next and the previous note, marking it on the page and in the Notes panel without opening it, and O opens it. Back returns to where you were before, once for all the notes gone through. Going to a page, turning to the next or the previous one (two pages side by side turn together), fitting the page and showing the panels have commands too, without keys until you give them some.
  • Keys for moving around the app. They are off unless you turn them on in Settings → Keyboard shortcuts: Alt+L for the library, Alt+P for papers, Alt+N for notes and one for each other page, Ctrl+, for Settings, Alt+B to narrow or widen the sidebar and Ctrl+Shift+F for the page's search box. While they are on, the sidebar names them. On a Mac they are ⌥ and ⌘ keys, and the ⌥ ones wait while you type, since ⌥ types letters there.
  • Every shortcut can be changed. In Settings → Keyboard shortcuts the commands are grouped by where they work (writing notes, reading a paper, tabs and windows, anywhere, moving around the app, sketch boards, the timeline, web pages) and found by what they do or by their key. Click a key and press the new one. A command can have several keys, or none, and a key another command has is refused, with that command's name. Buttons and hints show the keys chosen. Duplicating a tab, moving it to a new window and closing the other tabs can be given keys too, and so can New note and New reference, which then work on any page. Alt+Left and Alt+Right no longer go back and forward while you type in a text box, as on a Mac already.
  • A list of the page's keys. Ctrl+/ (⌘/ on a Mac) lists the keys of the page you are on, from the same list as Settings, with a link there to change them.
  • Keys in web pages. The app's keys work while a web page in a tab has the keyboard, as chosen in Settings: every key for tabs and windows, and the keys for moving around when they are on. Ctrl+L goes to the address bar from the page and from the bar above it.
  • A choice of search site. Settings → Web links chooses the site a browser tab opens on and searches, and that a citation is looked up on: Google Scholar, OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar.
  • The window's title shows the version: Reference Manager 0.7.14.
  • The new icon everywhere. The installer shows the app's icon, and makes Windows show the new icon on the taskbar and the desktop: it kept the old one in its cache.

Bug fixes

  • One unreadable setting no longer resets the rest. When one setting could not be read (one a newer version saved another way, say), every setting went back to its default. Now only that one does.
  • Spaces where PDF lines end. Text taken from a PDF ran the words at the end of one line and the start of the next together ("end ofthe line") in a highlight's quote, and so in what was copied or brought into a note. Lines are now joined with a space, and a word a hyphen breaks at a line's end is joined again. Copying from a PDF with Ctrl+C gives the same text.
